The warmest fall ocean in CalCOFI's record

In early November 2026, CalCOFI's fall cruise found the upper ocean off Southern California further above normal than on any cruise in a record that goes back to 1949.

Every season since 1949, CalCOFI ships have returned to the same grid of stations off California and lowered instruments from the surface into the deep. Between 31 October and 7 November 2026, R/V Sally Ride worked 36 of them off Southern California.

Averaged over the upper 50 m (164 ft), the ocean was 4.5 °C (8.0 °F) warmer than the 1993–2013 fall average at the stations sampled. That is far above anything measured during the 2014–2016 Blob marine heatwave and El Niño: the previous high over the same stations, in fall 2015, was 2.7 °C (4.8 °F).

The warmth reached unusually deep. About 12 km (7 miles) off northern San Diego County, water of 25 °C (77 °F) extended down to 55 m (180 ft), where the fall average is about 13 °C (55 °F). At 50 to 60 m that is 11.9 °C (21.3 °F) above normal, the largest departure CalCOFI has measured at any station and depth.

The warm water is also unusually salty, the mark of water carried in from the subtropics rather than warmed where it lies. NOAA reports a strong El Niño under way on top of a marine heatwave that has sat off California since 2025, and says the two cannot yet be told apart.

These are preliminary data, processed at sea. They have been checked against the instrument's second temperature sensor, water samples analysed on board, nearby buoys, the Scripps Pier shore station and satellite measurements.
